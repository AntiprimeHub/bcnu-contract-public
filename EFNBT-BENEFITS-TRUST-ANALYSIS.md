# The EFNBT vs. 100% Employer-Paid Benefits: What the Benefits-Trust Shift Means for Nurses

*A non-partisan analysis of the 2025–2029 Nurses' Bargaining Association settlement's move to an Employer-Funded Nurses' Benefits Trust (EFNBT), set against the documented worldwide track record of jointly-trusteed union benefit trusts.*

**Prepared:** 2026-05-31 · **Settlement executed:** May 21, 2026 · **EFNBT effective:** April 1, 2027

## TL;DR (plain language)

Until now, BC nurses had an unusual deal: the employer paid **100% of whatever the benefits plan cost, no matter how high** — a self-insured, open-ended promise with **no premiums** from nurses ([BCNU, *Update*, Dec 2019](https://www.bcnu.org/news-and-events/update-magazine/2019/dec2019-4-health-benefits)). The new agreement moves those benefits (Extended Health, Dental, Life, AD&D, and Long-Term Disability) into a **jointly-trusteed Employer-Funded Nurses' Benefits Trust (EFNBT)** on April 1, 2027 (🅑 `contracts/nba-2025-2029/changes/moa-health-and-welfare-benefits.md`, items 1–9, PDF p.179).

**What changes:** The employer's promise stops being "pay the bill, whatever it is" and becomes **"contribute a fixed percentage of payroll"** (3.90% + actual usage for the first two years, then 14.00%, then 14.25%) (🅑 `moa-efnbt-funding-formula.md`, items 15–19, PDF p.196). The trust — not the employer — now holds the money and bears the risk that benefits cost more than the contributions bring in.

**What members get in exchange:** union-appointed trustees gain **control over plan design** (exclusive authority over the non-LTD plans from 2029), a **$175 million union seed contribution** for long-term stability, an LTD risk-sharing formula that keeps the employer on the hook for disability cost spikes, and an **immediate menu of benefit upgrades** in 2027 (🅑 `moa-health-and-welfare-benefits-key-terms.md`, items 1–7).

**What members give up:** the open-ended employer guarantee. If, over time, costs outrun the fixed contribution formula and the trust's reserves, it is the **trust** that must close the gap — by drawing down reserves, or ultimately by trimming benefits — rather than an employer obligated to pay more. Importantly, and unlike *every other* BC health union's trust, **nurses still pay no premiums** into the EFNBT (🅑 key-terms doc; [BCNU, Dec 2019](https://www.bcnu.org/news-and-events/update-magazine/2019/dec2019-4-health-benefits)).

The net is a **classic trade-off: governance and design control, plus a funded reserve, in return for accepting that benefit security now depends on the adequacy of a funding formula and investment returns instead of an unlimited employer cheque.**

## 1. What actually changed (🅑 the EFNBT mechanics)

All facts in this section are from the settlement documents transcribed in this repo.

| Feature | Historic model (to Mar 31 2027) | EFNBT model (from Apr 1 2027) |
|---|---|---|
| Who holds the money | Employer self-insures; Pacific Blue Cross only *administers* | A standalone trust holds assets and pays claims |
| Employer's obligation | Pay 100% of actual plan cost, open-ended | Pay a **fixed % of payroll** into the trust |
| Who bears cost-inflation / investment risk | Employer | The **trust** (its reserves, the $175M seed, and the funding formula) |
| Member premiums | None | **None** (still employer-funded) |
| Who controls plan design | Changed only by collective bargaining | Union trustees (non-LTD, from 2029); LTD changed jointly |
| Governance | Employer is sole trustee/administrator | Trustees appointed by **both** NBA and HEABC |

**The funding formula** (🅑 `moa-efnbt-funding-formula.md`, items 15–21, PDF pp.196–197):
- **Apr 1 2027 – Mar 31 2029:** 3.90% of payroll **plus** the actual annual Extended-Health/Dental/Life/AD&D usage rate, reconciled to actuals each year (employers pay the *full actual cost* of these benefits for the first two years).
- **Apr 1 2029 – Mar 31 2031:** 14.00% of payroll.
- **Apr 1 2031 onward:** 14.25% of payroll.
- From Apr 1 2029, employers separately cover administrative expenses above 0.2% of payroll.
- **LTD risk-sharing:** a baseline disability-cost rate of 3.90% of payroll is set; if actual LTD usage exceeds it, employers make graduated additional contributions (more is shared as the overage grows past 2% and past 4%) — so the employer continues to absorb much of the disability-experience risk rather than passing it straight to the trust.

**Governance and the $175M** (🅑 `moa-health-and-welfare-benefits.md` items 7–8; `…-key-terms.md` items 1, 7):
- The NBA contributes **$175 million, one-time**, not drawn upon until after March 31, 2029, "for the long-term sustainability of the EFNBT."
- **NBA-appointed trustees have *exclusive* authority** to amend the Extended-Health, Dental, Life, and AD&D plans **beginning April 1, 2029**. LTD plan changes are made **jointly** by NBA and HEABC trustees. Before April 1, 2029, *any* change needs both parties' agreement.
- On commencement, the EFNBT must provide benefits **equivalent to the existing HBT plan as of May 20, 2026**, plus an immediate slate of upgrades (e.g., Group Life and AD&D raised from $50,000 to $250,000; the $25 Extended-Health deductible removed; mental-health paramedical doubled to $1,800/yr; massage set at $4,000/member) (🅑 `…-key-terms.md` item 2).

> ⚠️ **One nuance to keep honest about the word "open-ended."** "Open-ended" describes *who bears the cost* (the employer covered 100% of whatever the negotiated plan cost), **not** unlimited benefit ceilings. Design limits — deductibles, paramedical caps — always existed; they were simply changeable only through bargaining ([BCNU, Dec 2019](https://www.bcnu.org/news-and-events/update-magazine/2019/dec2019-4-health-benefits)).

## 2. The historical context most analyses miss: BCNU spent a decade *refusing* a trust

This is the single most important piece of context, and it comes straight from the prior (still in-force) agreement.

🅑 **Appendix U of the 2022–2025 agreement** (`current/sections/appendix-u-memorandum-of-agreement-sustainability-of-nba-benefits-plans.md`) records that:
- "In order to manage the rising costs of benefits HEABC has negotiated a Joint Benefit Trust with **every health sector bargaining association, other than the NBA**."
- "The NBA has maintained a position … that the BCNU … **would not agree to a Joint Health Benefit Trust**."
- The stated reason: "The Trust would require nurses to be **responsible for up to a 50% of future increased benefit cost**. This was and continues to be an **unacceptable proposition** for the NBA."

This is corroborated externally. BCNU's own magazine confirms the nurses' plan was "one of the only nurses' contracts where the employer pays 100 percent of the costs," and that the union "**once again resisted employer attempts to move nurses into a JBT**" ([BCNU, *Update*, Dec 2018](https://www.bcnu.org/news-and-events/update-magazine/2018/dec2018-3-flexible-benefits); [Dec 2019](https://www.bcnu.org/news-and-events/update-magazine/2019/dec2019-4-health-benefits)). 🌐 HEABC confirms the other subsectors moved on April 1, 2017: "Effective ongoing from April 1, 2017, benefits pursuant to the **Health Science Professionals, Community and Facilities** subsector collective agreements are provided by Joint Benefits Trusts (JBTs)," in which "union appointed trustees and employer appointed trustees are **equal partners**" ([HEABC](https://www.heabc.bc.ca/Page4544.aspx)).

**Why this matters for a neutral read of the EFNBT:** the thing the NBA objected to for over a decade was **member cost-sharing** — the JBT feature that pushes a share of cost overruns onto plan members. The EFNBT is explicitly engineered to *avoid that specific feature*: it borrows the **joint-trusteeship governance structure** of the JBTs but **not the member-premium / member-cost-share feature** ([BCNU, Dec 2019](https://www.bcnu.org/news-and-events/update-magazine/2019/dec2019-4-health-benefits); 🅑 key-terms doc). Whether the fixed-percentage-of-payroll formula re-introduces the same risk *indirectly* — by capping employer dollars so that any long-run shortfall lands on the trust's benefits rather than on members' paycheques — is the central open question (see §8).

## 3. In plain language: what does this mean for an individual member?

**Security of benefits.**
- *Before:* as secure as the employer's ability to pay — effectively a public-sector employer guaranteeing 100% of cost ([BCNU, Dec 2019](https://www.bcnu.org/news-and-events/update-magazine/2019/dec2019-4-health-benefits)).
- *After:* as secure as the trust's funding formula + reserves + investment returns. 🌐 In a funded trust, benefit security is a function of whether contributions and assets keep pace with claims. The U.S. health-and-welfare data shows this can be comfortable: the average multiemployer health fund held enough net assets to pay **about 14.5 months of benefits** ([Milliman, 2024](https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/multiemployer-health-and-welfare-fund-statistics-2024)). But it is *conditional* security, not a guarantee.

**Who bears cost-inflation risk?** This is the core shift. 🌐 Multiemployer/jointly-trusteed plans are "funded primarily by employer contributions … and by investment earnings" ([IFEBP](https://www.ifebp.org/resources---news/toolkits/understanding-multiemployer-plans)). Once the employer's obligation is a fixed contribution, **the trust absorbs the gap** when claims or medical inflation outrun the formula — except for LTD, where the BCNU formula keeps the employer sharing disability-experience risk (🅑 `moa-efnbt-funding-formula.md` items 21–23).

**Who controls plan design?** 🌐 In trust models, "depending on the trust agreement, the board either sets plan design and benefit levels or merely collects sufficient contributions to fund CBA-specified benefits" ([IFEBP](https://www.ifebp.org/resources---news/toolkits/understanding-multiemployer-plans)). 🅑 The EFNBT puts *non-LTD design in the union's hands* from 2029 — a genuine increase in member/union control compared to "change it only by bargaining."

**Surplus years vs. deficit years.**
- *Surplus:* the trust can enhance benefits or build reserves. 🌐 Crucially, the money **cannot go back to the employer**: under Canadian health-trust tax rules, "the funds of the trust and any income earned in the trust **cannot revert to the employer** or be used for a purpose other than providing health and welfare benefits" ([CRA Folio S2-F1-C1, ¶1.15](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/technical-information/income-tax/income-tax-folios-index/series-2-employers-employees/series-2-employers-employees-folio-1-specific-plans-offered-employers-employees/income-tax-folio-s2-f1-c1-health-welfare-trusts.html)). *(See source note in §10 — this is the legacy HWT folio; the anti-reversion principle is well-established and carries into the successor ELHT framework.)*
- *Deficit:* the trust draws on reserves (including the $175M seed after 2029); if a shortfall persists, trustees face the choice of trimming benefits — the risk the open-ended employer model did not place on members.

## 4. 🌐 Documented benefits of the jointly-trusteed trust model

1. **Member/union voice in plan design.** A multiemployer plan is governed by a "joint board of trustees consisting of equal representation from labor and management" who are the plan's "named fiduciary" ([IFEBP](https://www.ifebp.org/resources---news/toolkits/understanding-multiemployer-plans); [PBGC, *Introduction to Multiemployer Plans*](https://www.pbgc.gov/employers-practitioners/multiemployer/introduction)). The EFNBT goes *further* than the parity norm, giving union trustees exclusive authority over non-LTD design (🅑).
2. **Strong anti-reversion protection.** Trust assets and income cannot revert to the employer, and on wind-up any remaining funds must go to employees or to a registered charity — not back to the employer ([CRA Folio S2-F1-C1, ¶1.15 & ¶1.17](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/technical-information/income-tax/income-tax-folios-index/series-2-employers-employees/series-2-employers-employees-folio-1-specific-plans-offered-employers-employees/income-tax-folio-s2-f1-c1-health-welfare-trusts.html)).
3. **A funded reserve cushion.** Unlike pay-as-you-go employer funding, a trust accumulates assets. Across ~1,259 U.S. Taft-Hartley health funds (~5.2M members), the average could pay ~14.5 months of benefits from net assets, and ~65% held more than 12 months' worth ([Milliman, 2024](https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/multiemployer-health-and-welfare-fund-statistics-2024)).
4. **Professional, dedicated fund management** and the ability to add/upgrade benefits from pooled funds — visible in the EFNBT's immediate 2027 enhancement slate (🅑 key-terms doc, item 2).
5. **Mature trust infrastructure already exists in BC.** The not-for-profit **Healthcare Benefit Trust (HBT)** already administers exactly these benefit categories — "Group Life, AD&D, Weekly Indemnity, Long Term Disability (LTD), Extended Health, and Dental" — for ~130,000 BC healthcare employees across 300+ employers ([HBT](https://www.hbt.ca/about/)). The EFNBT is not building capacity from scratch.

## 5. 🌐 Documented drawbacks and risks

1. **Shortfalls land on the fund, and ultimately on benefits.** When a funded trust runs short — because contributions or investment returns fall behind claims — the fund, not an open-ended employer, must close the gap. In the U.S. multiemployer system, an insolvent plan first cuts benefits to a "**resource benefit level**" — "the highest level of benefits that the plan can pay out of the plan's assets" — and any government backstop covers only a **legally capped floor**, not the full promised benefit ([PBGC, *Plan Insolvency*](https://www.pbgc.gov/about/factsheets/page/plan-insolvency); [PBGC, *Multiemployer Benefit Guarantees*](https://www.pbgc.gov/prac/multiemployer/multiemployer-benefit-guarantees)).
   > ⚠️ **Transferability caveat — this is the biggest one.** Those mechanics are from U.S. *pension* plans under ERISA/PBGC. **There is no Canadian/BC equivalent backstop for health-and-welfare benefits**, and the insolvency *drivers* (many employers, employer withdrawal/bankruptcy) apply only weakly to the EFNBT, which is funded through a single employer association (HEABC). The example shows the *general principle* of risk-shift, **not** a predicted EFNBT outcome.
2. **Investment/market exposure.** The reserve cushion is exposed to markets. Milliman found the average health fund's months-of-reserves **fell** year-over-year "due to lower investment returns, despite expenses decreasing" ([Milliman, 2024](https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/multiemployer-health-and-welfare-fund-statistics-2024)). The same study shows ~17.6% of plans held under 6 months of reserves — the average masks weaker outliers.
3. **Fixed contributions can lag medical inflation.** A percentage-of-payroll formula rises with wages and headcount, not with health-cost trend. If benefit costs grow faster than payroll, the structural gap accrues to the trust.
4. **The cautionary macro example: the U.S. multiemployer *pension* crisis.** By the late 2000s, underfunding in multiemployer/Taft-Hartley pension plans became severe enough that Congress ultimately enacted a multibillion-dollar federal rescue (the **Special Financial Assistance program** under the American Rescue Plan Act of 2021) to keep insolvent plans paying benefits ([PBGC, *American Rescue Plan Act of 2021*](https://www.pbgc.gov/american-rescue-plan-act-of-2021)). ⚠️ Again: pensions, not health benefits, and U.S.-specific — but it is the clearest documented case of fixed-contribution trust funding failing at scale.
5. **Governance and fiduciary complexity.** Trustees are fiduciaries with personal legal duties. The EFNBT's *asymmetric* design (union-exclusive control of non-LTD plans) departs from the equal-parity Taft-Hartley/JBT norm; whether that asymmetry creates distinct governance or fiduciary-liability exposure is unresolved (see §8).

## 6. 🌐 How the model has actually panned out — nursing and non-nursing, worldwide

**BC health unions (the closest analogue — non-nursing).** Health Science Professionals, Community, and Facilities subsectors moved to Joint Benefits Trusts on April 1, 2017 ([HEABC](https://www.heabc.bc.ca/Page4544.aspx)). The Health Sciences Association frames the transition as protective — "benefits protected" as the sector "transitions to Joint Benefits Trust" ([HSABC](https://hsabc.org/news/benefits-protected-hspba-transitions-joint-benefits-trust)). The Joint Community Benefits Trust (JCBT) and Joint Facilities/Health-Science trusts are Employee Life & Health Trusts established that date ([JCBT](https://www.jcbt.ca/about-jcbt/); [JHSBT history](https://www.jhsbt.ca/about-jhsbt/history/)). The Joint Facilities Benefits Trust is explicit that it "receives negotiated funding from employers **and some contributions from plan members**" ([JFBT](https://www.jfbt.ca/about-jfbt/)) — the cost-sharing feature the EFNBT avoids.
   > ⚠️ **Evidence gap:** I did **not** find verified public solvency/reserve data, or any record of benefit cuts, for BC's own JCBT/JFBT/JHSBT/HBT since 2017. That would be the *most directly transferable* evidence and it remains an open question (§8). Absence of evidence here is not evidence of absence.

**Nursing specifically.** Beyond BC, I did not surface a well-documented, independently-verified nursing-union benefit-trust solvency case study. The honest statement is that the **directly nursing-specific empirical record is thin** in public sources; most transferable evidence is from broader health-and-welfare and multiemployer data, not nurse-specific funds.

**U.S. Taft-Hartley health & welfare funds (non-nursing, large-N).** The aggregate picture is "adequately reserved but market-exposed": ~14.5 months average reserves, declining with investment returns, with a meaningful minority thinly funded ([Milliman, 2024](https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/multiemployer-health-and-welfare-fund-statistics-2024)).

**U.S. multiemployer pensions (non-nursing, cautionary).** The clearest case of fixed-contribution trust funding breaking down — culminating in the 2021 federal Special Financial Assistance bailout ([PBGC](https://www.pbgc.gov/american-rescue-plan-act-of-2021)). Insolvency mechanics: benefit cuts to a resource level, then a capped statutory floor ([PBGC](https://www.pbgc.gov/about/factsheets/page/plan-insolvency)).

**The auto industry / UAW VEBA (non-nursing, the canonical risk-shift).** In 2007, GM, Ford, and Chrysler established Voluntary Employees' Beneficiary Association trusts to move a combined **~$80 billion** of retiree health-care liabilities off their balance sheets, funding the union-aligned **UAW Retiree Medical Benefits Trust**, which from 2010 covered ~675,000–860,000 retirees and dependents ([AARP, *The UAW VEBA*](https://assets.aarp.org/rgcenter/econ/i4_veba.pdf); [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2007/10/why-gms-transfer-its-health-plan-uaw-gamble)). ⚠️ "Union-controlled" overstates it — the trust is run by a committee of union *and* court-approved independent members and is "not operated by the UAW." This is the textbook employer-to-funded-trust risk shift the EFNBT echoes, and it is a retiree *health* trust (closer to the EFNBT's benefit mix than the pension cases).

**Canada, broader.** Jointly-sponsored / target-benefit trust models have expanded — e.g., the CAAT Pension Plan's "DBplus" has rapidly added hundreds of participating employers ([CAAT](https://www.caatpension.ca/news/caat-pension-plan-welcomes-over-340-new-employers-to-the-plan-in)) — evidence that jointly-trusteed risk-sharing vehicles are a growing, mainstream Canadian structure, though this is pensions, not health benefits.

## 7. ⚠️ How the EFNBT *differs* from the precedents (don't over-read the analogies)

1. **No member premiums.** BC's existing JBTs (and many Taft-Hartley funds) involve member cost-sharing; the EFNBT explicitly does **not** ([BCNU, Dec 2019](https://www.bcnu.org/news-and-events/update-magazine/2019/dec2019-4-health-benefits); 🅑 key-terms doc). This is the EFNBT's defining protective feature versus the model the NBA spent a decade rejecting.
2. **Asymmetric governance.** Standard Taft-Hartley/JBT governance is equal labour/management parity ([IFEBP](https://www.ifebp.org/resources---news/toolkits/understanding-multiemployer-plans); [HEABC](https://www.heabc.bc.ca/Page4544.aspx)). The EFNBT gives **NBA trustees exclusive** authority over non-LTD plans (🅑) — more union control than the norm.
3. **Single employer association, not many employers.** Funded through HEABC, so the "contributing-employer withdrawal / bankruptcy" insolvency drivers central to the U.S. multiemployer cases apply only weakly.
4. **Continuing employer risk-sharing on LTD.** The funding formula keeps employers absorbing disability-experience spikes (🅑 `moa-efnbt-funding-formula.md` items 21–23) — a hedge against the single most volatile benefit line.

Net: the precedents establish the **governance-model and risk-shift principles**, not the EFNBT's precise risk profile.

## 8. Net assessment (neutral) — conditions that protect members vs. erode benefits

**Members are well-protected when:**
- the contribution formula is set with conservative actuarial margin (the deal cites "actuarial analysis" and a $175M seed; 🅑 key-terms items 3, 7);
- reserves are built and maintained above ~12 months of expenses (the U.S. benchmark — [Milliman](https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/multiemployer-health-and-welfare-fund-statistics-2024));
- investment returns hold up and trustees govern prudently;
- anti-reversion and trust-law protections hold (assets stay locked for members — [CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/technical-information/income-tax/income-tax-folios-index/series-2-employers-employees/series-2-employers-employees-folio-1-specific-plans-offered-employers-employees/income-tax-folio-s2-f1-c1-health-welfare-trusts.html)).

**Members are exposed to erosion when:**
- benefit-cost trend persistently outpaces the 14.00–14.25%-of-payroll formula;
- investment returns disappoint and reserves thin (the documented mechanism behind U.S. fund-reserve declines — [Milliman](https://www.milliman.com/en/insight/multiemployer-health-and-welfare-fund-statistics-2024); and, at the extreme, the pension-insolvency pathway — [PBGC](https://www.pbgc.gov/about/factsheets/page/plan-insolvency));
- there is **no employer top-up obligation or backstop** once the formula is met — in which case the only levers are reserves or benefit cuts.

**Bottom line:** the EFNBT trades an **unconditional but employer-controlled** promise for a **conditional but union-influenced and funded** one. For members, the upside is real governance power, a dedicated reserve, immediate 2027 benefit gains, and continued zero premiums; the downside is that the *worst-case* now falls on the plan rather than on an employer obligated to keep paying. Whether that worst case ever materializes depends on the funding formula's adequacy, investment performance, and trustee stewardship — none of which can be assessed before the EFNBT operates.

## 9. Open questions / what we genuinely don't know

1. **What governs an EFNBT shortfall?** If the fixed contributions + $175M + LTD bands prove insufficient, is there any contractual employer top-up or backstop, or do members ultimately absorb benefit reductions? *(Not resolved in the settlement text reviewed.)*
2. **How have BC's own JBTs/HBT actually performed** on solvency and benefit stability since 2017? This is the most transferable evidence and was **not** found in public verified sources.
3. **Does the union-exclusive non-LTD authority create distinct fiduciary/governance risk** relative to the equal-parity norm?
4. **Will the EFNBT be an ELHT under ITA s.144.1** (like BC's other health trusts), conferring the same anti-reversion/tax protections assumed here by analogy?

**Source-quality & verification notes:**
- The **CRA folio URL returned HTTP 403 to automated re-fetch on 2026-05-31** (bot-blocking of a stable government page, not a dead link), but its text was **directly verified on 2026-05-31** against a manual copy of the page: ¶1.15 confirms the anti-reversion rule verbatim, and ¶1.17 confirms that on wind-up remaining funds go to employees or a registered charity. Note this is the **legacy Health and Welfare Trust (HWT)** folio: its administrative rules "no longer apply after December 31, 2021," and CRA intends to cancel the chapter on December 31, 2029. The HWT regime was replaced by the **Employee Life and Health Trust (ELHT)** framework (ITA s.144.1, ¶1.8); the anti-reversion protection carries forward, so the member protection survives even though the cited vehicle is the legacy one. BC's other health trusts (HBT, JCBT) are ELHTs, so the EFNBT would most plausibly be structured as one.
- BCNU magazine pages are **primary but union-framed**; used only for non-controversial descriptions of how the plans operate.
- **Two claims were refuted in verification and excluded:** a specific "4 employer + 4 employee" JCBT trustee split, and the assertion that equal union/employer representation is the *standard* CRA-recognized structure. Only HEABC's verified "equal partners" general description is used.
- The heaviest caveat throughout: **U.S. multiemployer *pension* evidence illustrates general principles, not directly applicable EFNBT outcomes** — there is no equivalent Canadian health-and-welfare backstop, and the EFNBT's single-employer-association structure weakens the U.S. insolvency drivers.
