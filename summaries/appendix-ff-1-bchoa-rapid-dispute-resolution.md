# Appendix FF.1 – BCHOA Rapid Dispute Resolution Process Trial — Gains & Losses
Source: `changes/appendix-ff-1-bchoa-rapid-dispute-resolution.md` · Baseline: none (new instrument)

**Tally:** ✅ 2 gains · ❌ 0 losses · ➖ 0 neutral · ⚠️ 3 watch-outs

## ✅ Gains
- **Hearings within ~1 month, decisions within 14 days** — new streamlined RDR track for non-complex Art. 9.02 grievances: Registrar endeavours to schedule within one (1) month of referral; arbitrator may rule orally on the day, otherwise written decision within 14 calendar days. Much faster than full arbitration. *(clauses E, G; PDF p.~?, see note below)*
- **Either party can opt out** — Union or HEABC may pull any rostered file deemed unsuitable for RDR at any time before hearing and send it back to normal case management/arbitration. Mutual-consent entry (clauses A, B) means no grievance is forced into the fast track. *(clauses A, B, D)*

## ⚠️ Watch-outs
- **Time-limited trial** — MOA takes effect on ratification and **terminates March 30, 2029** unless HEABC and NBA mutually agree to extend. The faster process is not permanent. *(final clause)*
- **No precedential value** — every RDR decision is "without prejudice," limited to the dispute at issue, and cannot be cited in any later proceeding. Speed comes at the cost of building usable case law for members. *(clause H)*
- **Stripped-down due process** — submissions capped at 10 minutes, **no witnesses**, case law limited to one submission, documentation kept minimal, and the Registrar can modify the rules. Fine for simple files; weak if a "non-complex" grievance turns out to need evidence. *(clause F)*
