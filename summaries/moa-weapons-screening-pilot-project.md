# MOA – Weapons Screening Pilot Project — Gains & Losses
Source: `changes/moa-weapons-screening-pilot-project.md` · Baseline: none (new instrument)

**Tally:** ✅ 2 gains · ❌ 0 losses · ➖ 1 neutral · ⚠️ 3 watch-outs

## ✅ Gains
- **New weapons-screening pilot** — First-time pilot of AI-based weapons detection and voluntary weapons screening as a proactive step against weapons-related violence in healthcare — a real safety gain at the pilot sites. *(MOA ¶1-3, PDF p.173)*
- **Dedicated funding** — Up to **$3.73 million** allocated from the OHS Fund (via Appendix A.1(A)) plus an additional **$100,000** for evaluation — funded, not aspirational. *(MOA ¶6, PDF p.173-174)*

## ➖ Neutral / Admin
- **Joint working group** — 4 Employer + 4 Union reps plus 1 Ministry of Health rep oversee the pilot and set evaluation metrics (cost-benefit, objects identified/surrendered, weapons incidents, injury rates, staff-perceived safety). Governance, not an entitlement. *(MOA ¶5, PDF p.173)*

## ⚠️ Watch-outs
- **Time-limited pilot, ~21 months** — Phase 1 planning (6 months) → Phase 2 implementation (1 year) → Phase 3 evaluation (within 3 months). Ends in a report only; no commitment to permanent rollout. *(MOA Phases 1-3, PDF p.173)*
- **Only 4 sites; screening is "voluntary" at 3 of them** — AI detection only at **Surrey Memorial (FHA)**; voluntary screening at **Vancouver General ED, UHNBC ED (Prince George), Kelowna General ED**. Most nurses see no change, and "voluntary" screening may limit protective effect. *(MOA Phase 2, PDF p.173)*
- **Expansion is only "discuss whether there is interest"** — adding further sites is not committed, just a commitment to talk about it. *(MOA ¶7, PDF p.174)*
